# Business Model

## Model archetype: Fund-backed venture studio (GP/LP)

EBDA Ventures is structured as a **fund-backed venture studio**. The studio is the General Partner of an LP-backed fund. Capital is raised from institutional and high-net-worth LPs and deployed into the studio's own spin-out companies. The studio captures value through both standard fund economics and operator-grade equity in each portfolio company.

**Why this model fits:**
- Aligns with [[emilio]]'s 30+ years of PE/VC fund-management experience — Emilio has raised, structured, and operated ~20 funds totaling €1B+ AUM. The fund-management motion is his core competence.
- Familiar structure for institutional LPs — easier diligence than a non-standard equity-for-services structure.
- Enables disciplined portfolio construction and consistent reporting (ILPA / IPEV / EVCA standards Emilio already operates to).

## Revenue / capture mechanics

Two-layer economics:

**Layer 1 — Fund economics (GP):**
- Management fee on committed capital (industry standard 2% range)
- Carried interest on fund returns above hurdle (industry standard 20% range)
- Exact terms TBD during PPM structuring

**Layer 2 — Studio equity at formation (operator-grade):**
- Studio (fund + GP combined) takes **35–55% equity at company formation** — a "balanced" studio stake
- Leaves substantial equity for the operating CEO and founding team (large enough to attract A-tier operators)
- Studio remains the largest single shareholder through seed; dilutes through Series A/B as growth capital comes in

This dual-layer structure is the central economic engine. Management fees pay for the studio's operating cost; carry plus the studio's direct equity in each spin-out drive long-term value.

## Fund I sizing

**Target: EUR 20–50M.**

This is a deliberately sized first fund — large enough to fund a credible studio pipeline, small enough to be raisable for a first-time studio GP and to deliver clean returns. With a ~€2–4M effective deployment per company at formation + seed, Fund I funds approximately **8–12 ventures**.

This is also the most honest framing for LP conversations: a EUR 20–50M Fund I does **not** fund 30 companies. That number (see below) is a multi-fund lifetime target.

## The 30–35 ventures framing

The aspirational 30–35 AI Healthcare ventures referenced in [[vision]] is a **multi-fund lifetime target**, not a Fund I commitment. The realistic decomposition:

- **Fund I** (~€20–50M): 8–12 ventures
- **Fund II** (~€75–150M): 10–15 ventures (assuming Fund I performance unlocks larger LP commitments)
- **Fund III+** (size TBD): the remainder

This framing matters for two reasons:
1. **LP diligence credibility.** A sophisticated LP will do the per-company math in 30 seconds. The studio must own the multi-fund framing upfront.
2. **Operational pacing.** Even top venture studios rarely sustain >6 launches per year. 8–12 over a Fund I vintage is achievable; 30 is not.

## Geographic focus: US-primary

EBDA Ventures targets the **US healthcare market** as its primary operating geography.

- **HQ and entity structure:** US-domiciled fund and GP (likely Delaware LP + Cayman feeder, depending on LP base; final structure TBD)
- **LP base:** Primarily US institutional and high-net-worth
- **Portfolio operating geography:** US healthcare systems, US clinics, US payors as initial customers
- **Primary regulatory frame:** **HIPAA and FDA** (see [[regulatory-watch]] *(planned)*)
- **Secondary regulatory frame:** EU AI Act and MDR remain relevant for any later European expansion of portfolio companies, but are not the Fund I primary

## Strategic tension: European founder, US-focused studio

[[emilio]] is European and has spent his career in European PE/VC. EBDA Ventures targets the US market. This is the **single biggest structural challenge** in the studio's setup, and it has to be solved explicitly:

- **US LP access:** Emilio's existing LP relationships are predominantly European. The US institutional LP base (endowments, family offices, fund-of-funds, healthcare strategics) requires either a co-GP based in the US, a US-based placement agent, or a multi-year US relationship-building effort before Fund I close.
- **US clinical and regulatory expertise:** HIPAA, FDA AI/ML, state-by-state healthcare regulation, and clinical operations in US health systems are not Emilio's core. Must be filled via US-based hires, US clinical advisors, and/or partnership with a US healthcare-specialist legal firm.
- **US deal flow:** Spin-out CEOs and EIRs should be predominantly US-based to access US clinical networks and US talent markets.

These are **not** reasons to abandon US-focus — the US is the largest, fastest, and best-capitalized AI healthcare market. But they are reasons to **structure the studio's team and partnerships around closing these gaps from day one.** This should be a central topic in [[hiring-plan]] *(planned)* and a key consideration in the LP pitch narrative.

## Open questions for downstream interviews

- Exact fund terms: management fee %, carry %, hurdle rate, GP commitment
- Studio operating budget — what does the management fee need to cover? (Drives min. fund size.)
- Co-GP question — does EBDA bring on a US-based co-GP, or stay sole-GP with US advisors?
- Anchor LP strategy — who is the realistic anchor for Fund I? (Healthcare strategic? Family office? Fund-of-funds?)
- Portfolio reserve policy — what % of Fund I held back for follow-on rounds in winners?
- Entity domicile — Delaware? Cayman? Spanish ManCo + US fund? Tax structuring needs specialist input.
