# Positioning

## The one-line position

**EBDA Ventures is the AI-native healthcare venture studio.** Every other US healthcare studio was built before the generative-AI inflection and is bolting AI onto a workflow-studio chassis. EBDA is built around AI capability — shared agentic infrastructure, RAG, voice, model monitoring, clinical-AI compliance — from the *first* venture forward.

## Primary comparable: Redesign Health

The single comparable LPs will use to frame EBDA is **Redesign Health** (NYC, founded 2018). Redesign is the dominant US healthcare venture studio: ~$1B+ raised across funds, 70+ companies launched, deep US healthcare distribution network, well-known LP base (General Catalyst, BlackRock, others). Notable spin-outs include Calibrate Health, Jasper Health, and Stellar Health.

**EBDA's answer to "why not just be Redesign?"** rests on three differences, in order of importance:

### 1. AI-native architecture vs. workflow-native architecture (primary wedge)

Redesign was built in 2018 when "healthcare workflow software" was the dominant playbook. Its shared infrastructure is healthcare-distribution-first: clinical advisors, payor relationships, regulatory templates, GTM motions. AI was added later, layered onto a workflow-studio chassis.

EBDA inverts the architecture. The shared infrastructure is AI-first:
- Shared AI platform: agents, RAG, voice, clinical documentation, analytics
- EHR/CRM/ERP integration scaffolding
- AI-specific compliance: model monitoring, auditability, clinical risk frameworks for AI/ML systems
- Foundation-model vendor relationships and shared inference costs

Clinical and regulatory infrastructure exists too — but it's organized *around* an AI-native operating model, not the reverse. See [[vision]] (Force 3: AI capability is now production-grade) and [[market-thesis]] for the macro framing.

### 2. Disease-vertical focus vs. generalist healthcare (supporting wedge)

Redesign launches across the entire healthcare surface: employer benefits, payor ops, provider workflow, RCM, mental health, fertility, etc. The breadth is a strength (more shots on goal) and a weakness (no concentrated thesis any one venture inherits).

EBDA narrows to four high-burden disease areas (**cardiovascular, obesity, women's diseases, oncology**) plus a transversal **AI Healthcare Copilots** layer (see [[vision]]). The implication: each EBDA spin-out inherits not just shared AI infrastructure but a focused clinical thesis the studio is repeatedly building expertise in. A second cardio venture launched in year 3 benefits from everything learned in years 1–2; a 70-company generalist portfolio gets less of that compounding.

### 3. Operator-friendly equity + fund-grade discipline (supporting wedge)

Redesign is well-known for taking heavy founder-grade equity. EBDA's 35–55% studio stake at formation (see [[business-model]]) leaves substantially more equity for the operating CEO and team. This is positioned to attract A-tier operating CEOs who would not take a heavy-studio role.

Coupled with [[emilio]]'s 30+ years of PE/VC fund-management discipline (ILPA/IPEV/EVCA reporting, valuation methods, portfolio monitoring), EBDA offers operators a combination most studios can't: meaningful operator equity *and* institutional-grade fund operations.

## Other comparables — context only

- **Flagship Pioneering** — biotech R&D studio (Moderna, etc.). Different domain. Useful as a counter-example: EBDA is not a science-discovery studio, it's a workflow-and-clinical-AI studio. LPs evaluating Flagship are not the same as LPs evaluating EBDA.
- **Atomic** — generalist heavy-studio model (~80% founding equity). Touches healthcare occasionally. Useful for the operator-equity contrast: Atomic is the canonical "heavy" studio EBDA is positioning *against*.
- **AlleyCorp** — NYC multi-sector studio with healthcare bets. Generalist; doesn't claim AI healthcare specialization. Not a primary comparable.
- **General Catalyst Health Assurance / Healthcare Transformation funds** — not a studio, but the dominant *narrative-shaper* in AI healthcare investing. LPs who back GC will frame EBDA in GC's vocabulary; EBDA differentiates by being a builder, not just a capital provider.
- **a16z Bio + Health** — similar framing concern as GC: a category-shaping VC, not a studio competitor. Useful as a narrative anchor in LP pitches ("we're building the studio that supplies the next wave of companies a16z and GC will want to back at Series A").

## The narrative wedge for LP conversations

When an LP asks *why not just back Redesign?*, the EBDA answer is:

> Redesign is the right studio for the *previous* generation of healthcare companies — workflow-first, distribution-led, AI-added. EBDA is the right studio for the *next* generation, where the company is AI-native from day one, the disease focus is specific, and operator equity is structured to recruit A-tier clinical-AI builders. The market window for that thesis (2025–2028, per [[market-thesis]]) favors a focused studio entering now over a generalist studio diversifying late.

## The honest weakness

EBDA is **smaller, newer, and less networked** than Redesign. Fund I targets EUR 20–50M against Redesign's $1B+ across funds. EBDA does not have Redesign's seven years of US health-system relationships. And the European-founder / US-studio gap (see [[business-model]]) means EBDA's US distribution must be deliberately constructed rather than inherited.

The positioning therefore must be **sharpness over scale**. EBDA cannot win by being a smaller Redesign. It must win by being *unmistakably the AI-native, disease-focused, operator-friendly* alternative — and by recruiting US co-GP / clinical-advisor / EIR talent that closes the distribution gap.

## Risks to this positioning

- **AI-native becomes table stakes.** If by 2027 every healthcare studio claims to be AI-native, the wedge erodes. Mitigation: build provable shared AI infrastructure (not just marketing) and document it.
- **Redesign repositions.** Redesign has the resources to rebrand as AI-native and outspend on marketing. Mitigation: lead with portfolio proof points, not category claims.
- **Disease-vertical focus narrows TAM.** Four disease areas plus copilots may strike LPs as too narrow if early spin-outs don't perform. Mitigation: frame as a *starting* portfolio with explicit option to expand to adjacent disease areas in Fund II+.
