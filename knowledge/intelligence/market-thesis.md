# AI Healthcare Market Thesis

This file holds the **evidence layer** behind [[vision]]. Vision states the bet in narrative form; this file holds the numbers, sources, and signals that justify it. Update this file when new data points materially strengthen or weaken the thesis.

## Force 1 — Structural demand

**WHO health-worker shortfall.** The World Health Organization projects an ~11 million health-worker shortfall by 2030. Health systems at every income level face deployment, retention, and performance issues. Implication: healthcare cannot solve its capacity problem through hiring — only through automation, triage, documentation support, workflow orchestration, and clinical productivity tools.

## Force 2 — Financial waste in workflows

**U.S. administrative waste.** Peterson Health Technology Institute estimates ~$350B/year in U.S. healthcare administrative waste, of which **~$266B is tied to administrative complexity**. This defines the addressable surface for AI:

- Documentation
- Billing
- Prior authorization
- Claims processing
- Scheduling
- Patient communication
- Back-office

These are the workflow targets where ROI is most measurable — the selection rule used in [[vision]].

## Force 3 — AI capability is production-grade

**McKinsey Q4 2025 healthcare AI survey:**

- ~50% of healthcare organizations have implemented generative AI (up from ~25% in late 2023 — a 2x in roughly two years)
- 100% of respondents have at least *some* AI plans
- 19% are already in implementation maturity on **agentic AI**
- 51% are running **proofs of concept** on agentic AI

Implication: the buyer side has moved from curiosity to deployment. The "wait and see" stance is no longer the default.

## Force 4 — Capital is rewarding workflow-ROI bets

**Rock Health 2025 funding data:**

- U.S. digital health startups raised **$14.2B in 2025**, +35% YoY
- **AI-enabled** digital health companies captured **54% of total funding**, up from 37% the prior year

**Reference deals (validation signals):**

- **Abridge** — raised $250M for AI medical documentation; product deployed in ~100 U.S. health systems. Demonstrates that provider-facing AI workflow tools can reach institutional scale.
- **Isomorphic Labs** — raised $2.1B for AI-driven drug design. Demonstrates that AI healthcare extends beyond software/ops into R&D, pharma, and biotech infrastructure.

## Regulatory landscape

**United States — FDA AI/ML medical-device list.** The FDA maintains a public list of AI-enabled medical devices intended to give innovators insight into the device landscape and regulatory expectations. Implication: AI in regulated healthcare is becoming a defined category, no longer a grey zone.

**European Union — AI Act.** Entered into force in 2024 with phased implementation. High-risk AI rules and regulatory sandboxes are becoming increasingly relevant for healthcare deployments. Creates complexity, but also creates an **execution advantage for teams that understand the regulation early** — a fit for a venture studio model that builds shared regulatory infrastructure once.

## Why these four forces converging matters

Any one of these forces alone would be interesting. The thesis is that the **convergence** creates a finite window (roughly 2025–2028) where:

- Buyers are open to spending
- Capital is open to funding
- Regulators are clarifying rules
- AI capability is finally good enough

A venture studio with healthcare distribution, regulatory discipline, and deep AI execution is well-positioned to compound across that window in a way a single startup is not.

## Open questions / unknowns to track

- Will the McKinsey 50% adoption figure plateau or keep accelerating? (Watch the 2026 update.)
- Does Rock Health funding stay weighted toward AI-enabled in 2026, or does AI become table-stakes (and thus stop being a differentiator)?
- How does the EU AI Act's high-risk regime concretely apply to clinical AI agents — and where do the regulatory sandboxes actually open?
- Reimbursement: which workflow AI tools are getting CPT codes or being reimbursed by payors? (Currently a gap in our evidence.)
