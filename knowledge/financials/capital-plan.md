## Why "personal capital + family office anchor" rather than other paths

The combination is internally consistent and plays to Emilio's specific situation:

1. **Personal capital avoids the chicken-and-egg problem.** A pre-fund warehouse facility from an anchor LP requires the anchor in hand; healthcare strategic operating capital cedes optionality before the studio has any. Personal capital removes external dependencies during the most fragile 12 months.

2. **Family office anchors fit a Spain-based sole-GP profile.** Family offices make decisions on personal relationships and conviction, not institutional process. Emilio's 30+ years of fundraising relationships (see [[emilio]]) are disproportionately with HNW principals and family offices rather than US endowments and pension funds — this matches his network strengths.

3. **Faster cycle.** Family office due diligence typically runs 3–6 months versus 9–18 months for institutional. A Q2 2027 first close is achievable with a family office anchor and very hard with an institutional anchor.

4. **Trades institutional credibility for execution speed.** EBDA cannot win Fund I on institutional credibility (first-time studio, EU founder, US market). It can win on personal-relationship credibility + clear thesis + visible team. Family offices reward that combination; institutions don't.

The trade-off: Fund II must upgrade to institutional LPs (FoF + endowments + larger strategics) to scale beyond EUR 50M. The Fund I LP base should be selected with that progression in mind — favor family offices that will themselves re-up in Fund II and that have credibility in the LP-of-LPs world.

## Fund terms (proposed defaults)

To be finalized during PPM structuring with fund counsel. Defaults below follow industry norms for first-time venture studio funds:

| Term | Proposed default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Management fee** | 2% of committed capital | Industry standard for venture; studios sometimes go slightly higher (2.25–2.5%) to fund operating infrastructure; resist if possible to maintain LP-friendliness |
| **Carried interest** | 20% above hurdle | Industry standard |
| **Preferred return (hurdle)** | 8% | Standard for venture; some studios negotiate 6% given the operating intensity |
| **GP catch-up** | 100% | Standard |
| **GP commitment** | 1–2% of Fund I | Standard; Emilio's personal capital pre-close counts toward this |
| **Investment period** | 4 years | Standard for venture |
| **Fund term** | 10 years + 2 × 1-year extensions | Standard |
| **Distribution waterfall** | European (whole-fund) | More LP-friendly than American (deal-by-deal); appropriate for a first-time fund |
| **Key-person provision** | Triggered if Emilio + 1 other senior key person both depart | Required given key-person risk per [[risks]] |
| **Recycling** | Up to 100% of called capital within investment period | Standard for venture; useful for studios given operating burn |

## Entity domicile (proposed structure — requires specialist confirmation)

Standard structure for a fund with mixed US/EU LP base, US-targeted investments, and a Spain-based GP:

- **Investing vehicle:** Delaware LP (standard US fund structure; FATCA-compliant; familiar to US LPs)
- **Non-US LP feeder:** Cayman LP feeder (avoids US tax exposure for non-US LPs)
- **GP entity:** Delaware LLC (general partner of the Delaware LP) OR Luxembourg structure (depending on Emilio's tax residence and EU LP structure preferences)
- **Management company (ManCo):** Spanish or Luxembourg ManCo, employing Emilio and the studio team; provides services to the GP entity

**This structure is provisional.** Final structuring requires:
- Specialist international fund tax counsel (US side: Goodwin, Cooley, K&L Gates, etc.; EU side: Arendt, Clifford Chance, etc.)
- Decision on Emilio's tax residence going forward (Spain vs. relocation)
- Decision on whether EBDA will register as a US Investment Adviser (likely required given US LP base)
- AIFMD considerations if any EU institutional LPs are involved

**Action item:** engage specialist fund counsel by Q3 2026 to finalize structure ahead of PPM drafting (Q4 2026 per [[hiring-plan]] timeline). Logged in [[ops/action-items|action-items]].

## Capital plan — sensitivity scenarios

The plan above is the **base case**. Worth modeling two stress scenarios explicitly:

**Slip scenario (12-month delay):**
- First close pushes to Q2 2028
- Pre-Fund I burn extends to ~EUR 4–6M (Emilio personal capital tested)
- Tier 1 hires may need to leave or accept reduced comp
- First spin-out pushes to 2029
- **Mitigation:** maintain a 6-month operating cash reserve; structure Tier 1 contracts with milestone-based components; consider downsize to EUR 15–25M fund if needed

**Headwind scenario (Fund I undersized):**
- First close lands at EUR 15M instead of EUR 20–50M target
- Fund I supports 5–7 ventures instead of 8–12
- Reserve policy must be conservative; less follow-on capacity
- **Mitigation:** narrow disease vertical focus initially (2 of 4 verticals + copilots, expand in Fund II); reduce platform engineering scope in v1

Both scenarios should be addressed in the PPM as honest "what could go wrong" framing — LPs reward this transparency.
