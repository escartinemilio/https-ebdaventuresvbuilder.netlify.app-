# Customer Segments

EBDA Ventures has two distinct customer surfaces, each with its own buying logic and sales motion. Both must be addressed deliberately — the studio's success depends on closing on both.

| Surface | Customer | Sold by | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Clinical buyers** (provider-side) | Each portfolio company, supported by studio shared GTM | Workflow ROI: hours saved, revenue recovered, throughput increased |
| B | **Limited Partners** | EBDA Ventures GP (Emilio + advisory board + placement agent) | Access to a curated AI healthcare portfolio with disciplined fund operations |

## Surface A — Clinical buyers (provider-side primary)

### Primary buyer types

EBDA's portfolio companies primarily sell into **provider-side buyers**, consistent with the workflow-ROI selection rule from [[vision]] (hours saved, revenue recovered, patients converted, claims processed, capacity increased).

| Buyer | Profile | Typical decision dynamics | Best-fit EBDA vertical |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Large integrated delivery networks (IDNs)** | Hospital systems with 10+ hospitals (HCA, Ascension, CommonSpirit, Kaiser, etc.) | 12–24 month sales cycles; VAC + IT-security review; centralized procurement | All four disease verticals (service-line-level entry); copilots at enterprise level |
| **Academic medical centers** | Stanford Health, Mass General Brigham, Johns Hopkins, Cleveland Clinic, etc. | Innovation-friendly; clinical validation partners; longer pilot phases | Disease verticals (research-grade clinical partners); good first-pilot sites |
| **Specialty clinic chains** | National or regional chains: cardiology practices, OB/GYN groups, oncology clinic networks, fertility chains | 6–12 month cycles; smaller IT footprint; outcome- and revenue-focused | Strong fit for disease verticals (focused buyer matches focused product) |
| **Mid-market community hospitals** | Single-hospital or small-system community health systems | 6–12 month cycles; resource-constrained; high need for capacity-multiplying AI | AI Copilots (documentation, scheduling, RCM); some disease-vertical |
| **Independent physician groups** | Larger physician-owned groups (200+ providers) | 3–9 month cycles; high physician influence; ROI-driven | Disease verticals + clinic-level copilots |

### Secondary buyer surfaces

Selected portfolio companies may target adjacent buyers in addition to provider-side:

- **Payors** — for clinical decision-support tools, prior-authorization automation, and population-health products. Smaller buyer universe (top 20 payors capture most spend); longer sales cycles (12–24 months); larger contracts.
- **Employers (self-insured)** — for benefits-management AI, employee chronic-care management. Channel sales (through benefits brokers); less direct.
- **Pharma** — for clinical-trial operations tools. Different sales motion entirely; typically separate venture if pursued.

These are **not** the studio's primary focus. Individual portfolio companies may serve them; the studio's shared GTM infrastructure is being built around provider-side first.

### Buyer-side personas (to be deepened post-clinical-lead hire)

The clinical operating lead from [[hiring-plan]] (Tier 1 hire #2, target Q4 2026) will own the deepening of buyer personas per disease vertical. Initial high-level personas:

- **Chief Medical Information Officer (CMIO)** — primary champion for clinical AI adoption; cares about clinical outcomes, EHR integration, physician workflow disruption
- **Chief Operating Officer / VP Operations** — cares about throughput, capacity, cost-per-encounter
- **Chief Financial Officer / VP Revenue Cycle** — cares about revenue recovered, claims processed, billing accuracy
- **Service-line Chief (Cardiology Chief, Chief of Oncology, etc.)** — clinical sponsor for vertical-specific products; relationship-driven
- **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)** — gatekeeper for any AI/ML system handling PHI; HIPAA, model-monitoring, audit-logging requirements

### Shared GTM implications

EBDA's studio-level GTM infrastructure (from [[studio-playbook]]) is structured around provider-side selling:

- Pre-built IT security questionnaires for the AI platform's HIPAA + model-monitoring posture
- Standard VAC (Value Analysis Committee) presentation templates
- Reference architecture documentation for EHR integrations (Epic, Cerner, athena, eCW)
- Shared procurement-navigation playbook
- Portfolio-level reference customer development (one strong reference per disease vertical)

This shared infrastructure is what makes the "studio premium" real for an operating CEO — they inherit a provider-sales motion that would otherwise take 18 months to build from scratch.

## Surface B — Limited Partners (studio fundraising)

The studio itself sells one product: **access to a fund deploying capital + operating support into AI Healthcare ventures**. Sold by Emilio (with US advisory board support and placement agent), per [[capital-plan]].

### LP personas

Formalizing the LP types specified in [[capital-plan]]:

| LP type | Target % of Fund I | Profile | Buying logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family office (anchor)** | 25–40% | US-domiciled preferred; EU FOs with US healthcare interest acceptable; existing healthcare PE/VC allocation; check size EUR 5–20M | Relationship-driven; backs convicted operators with clear theses; values speed + flexibility over institutional process |
| **HNW investor syndicate** | 20–40% | 10–25 individuals; checks EUR 250K–2M; healthcare interest or AI interest; often introduced via Emilio's network or advisory board | Personal conviction + portfolio-diversification logic; values access to a curated portfolio they couldn't build themselves |
| **Healthcare strategics (optional)** | 10–25% | 1–2 strategic LPs: large health system, payor, pharma, or healthcare-tech corp | Strategic value (first look at portfolio, co-investment, channel access) > pure financial returns; brings distribution credibility |
| **Fund-of-funds / smaller institutional** | 0–15% in Fund I | FoFs specializing in venture/studio; smaller US institutional first-time-fund backers | Process-driven diligence; values team + thesis + governance; stretch goal for Fund I, base case for Fund II |
| **GP (Emilio)** | 1–2% | Standard GP commitment | Alignment with LPs; structurally required |

### LP buying objections (anticipated)

Three objections likely to come up repeatedly in LP conversations, and the prepared answers:

1. **"You're a first-time studio with no track record."**
   *Answer:* Track record is in fund management ([[emilio]]: 20 funds, EUR 1B+ AUM) — *operating risk* is being mitigated by hiring Tier 1 senior operators before LP outreach (per [[hiring-plan]]) and is the explicit reason for the conservative Fund I size.

2. **"European founder, US-focused studio — how do you actually have US distribution?"**
   *Answer:* US advisory board substantially formed before LP outreach (per [[hiring-plan]]); US Managing Director hired Q1 2027; clinical operating lead US-based and building per-vertical advisory boards. The gap is real and is being closed structurally, not papered over.

3. **"Why not just back Redesign Health?"**
   *Answer:* See [[positioning]]. Redesign is workflow-native with AI bolted on; EBDA is AI-native with disease-vertical focus. Different category, different vintage. We're the studio for the next generation of AI-first healthcare companies that need genuine AI infrastructure as much as healthcare distribution.

These objections are documented here so the answers are consistent across every LP conversation, advisory board introduction, and placement-agent pitch.

## Cross-surface coordination

The two surfaces are not independent — they reinforce each other:

- **Reference customers from Surface A become proof points for Surface B.** A signed pilot with a brand-name US health system in 2026 dramatically accelerates LP conversations in early 2027.
- **LP introductions from Surface B become customer access for Surface A.** Healthcare strategics as LPs (the optional 10–25% slot) can become Surface A buyers for portfolio companies.

The studio playbook explicitly cycles between these surfaces: each spin-out's customer wins become studio fundraising fuel, and each LP relationship becomes potential portfolio-customer introduction.
