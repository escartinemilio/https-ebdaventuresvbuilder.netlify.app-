## Flag — the consequence of the sole-GP choice

[[emilio]] elected to remain sole-GP rather than recruit a US-based co-GP (see [[business-model]] open question, now resolved). The cost saving is real (no carry dilution, faster decision-making) but it has two structural consequences that this hiring plan must compensate for:

1. **The US advisory board becomes load-bearing.** It is no longer "nice to have." It must include 2–3 senior US healthcare investor voices who lend Fund I real credibility with US LPs — not just generic medical KOLs.
2. **The placement agent choice becomes critical.** A weak placement agent + sole-GP + first-time-studio vehicle is the closest practical path to Fund I close failure (risk #1). The agent must be a US healthcare specialist with active institutional LP relationships.

Both of these are surfaced as separate workstreams below.

## US advisory board — composition (load-bearing per the sole-GP flag above)

Target composition: 6–8 advisors, formed and announced before Q1 2027 LP outreach.

| Type | Target count | Profile |
|---|---|---|
| **US healthcare investor advisors** | 2–3 | Former GPs / partners at General Catalyst Health Assurance, Define Ventures, Andreessen Horowitz Bio + Health, Foundation Health Partners, OPC, or similar. These names lend institutional credibility with US LPs. |
| **Clinical KOLs (one per disease vertical)** | 4 | Cardiologist / cardio-surgeon, obesity/metabolic disease specialist, women's health (likely OB/GYN or fertility), oncologist — all with US academic medical center + practicing affiliations. |

**Compensation:** Standard advisor equity (0.1–0.5% of studio GP equity); quarterly cadence; 1-year initial term.

## Placement agent selection — selection criteria

Engagement target: H2 2026, in time to support Q1 2027 LP outreach.

**Selection criteria (in priority order):**

1. **US healthcare specialization.** Generalist placement agents will not solve the EU→US gap. Must have actively placed US healthcare vehicles in the last 3 years.
2. **Active institutional LP relationships** in the size band relevant to a EUR 20–50M Fund I — likely family offices, healthcare strategics, smaller fund-of-funds, and high-net-worth syndicates rather than the largest endowments.
3. **Track record with first-time studio GPs.** First-time fund + studio model is a hard sell; specialized experience matters.
4. **Reasonable economics.** Standard placement-agent fee is 2% of capital raised, payable on close. Avoid agents demanding retainers without performance bands.

Candidate firms to evaluate (illustrative; to be researched in detail in a separate workstream): Park Hill, Atlantic-Pacific, Eaton Partners (Stifel), Triago, MVision, Capstone Partners. Confirm current healthcare-vehicle activity before approach.

## Pre-Fund I funding (critical open question)

Tier 1 hires need to be paid *before* Fund I closes. Total estimated burn from now to Q2 2027 first close (~12 months) at 5–6 Tier 1 senior hires + operating costs: **estimated EUR 2–4M**.

Funding options (to be resolved in [[capital-plan]] *(planned)*):

1. **Emilio's personal capital / GP commitment runway.** Cleanest but capital-intensive on Emilio personally.
2. **Pre-fund anchor commitment / warehouse facility.** A strong anchor LP commits early; advances draw against future Fund I close. Standard structure but requires anchor in hand.
3. **Healthcare strategic partner.** Sells a strategic right (first look at portfolio, co-investment access) in exchange for operating capital.
4. **Bridge financing from a syndicate.** Friends-and-family or angel syndicate funds the studio's operating runway in exchange for a slice of GP economics.

This decision blocks the entire hiring sequence and is the single most urgent item for [[capital-plan]].

## Compensation philosophy

- **Senior hires (Tier 1):** Below-market salary + meaningful equity in the studio GP entity + carried interest participation pool. Industry norm for studio Tier 1 is ~50–70% of equivalent senior corporate cash comp + 0.5–3% of GP carry depending on role.
- **EIRs:** Modest base salary (€100–180K USD-equivalent) + small studio equity + significant equity in their venture (target 15–25% of the venture post-spin-out, depending on funding stage).
- **Tier 3 and downstream:** Closer to market cash comp + standard equity grants.

Total comp philosophy is **equity-heavy, mission-led**: candidates who need top-of-market cash comp pre-product-launch are not the right fit for a first-time studio.

## Open questions

- **AI/ML lead profile.** Should the first AI/ML hire have a clinical-AI background specifically, or a general production-ML/agentic-AI background with healthcare interest? (Affects recruiting universe materially.)
- **In-house vs. fractional studio counsel.** Hiring decision depends on Tier 1 burn rate.
- **EIR sourcing.** Is the EIR pipeline built through Emilio's existing network, through the US advisory board, through targeted outbound, or through a public EIR program? Likely a mix — needs explicit channel strategy.
- **Compensation benchmark sources.** Specific salary bands should be set against credible market data (Korn Ferry, Pave, Levels.fyi for tech; specialized executive search firms for clinical roles).
- **Equity vesting cliff for Tier 1.** Standard 1-year cliff / 4-year vest, or shorter cliff to attract talent into a first-time studio?
